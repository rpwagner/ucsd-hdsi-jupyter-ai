## 3:00 - First half: Put a research notebook to work

### Intro and Recap

Get settled and carry forward questions from DataHub. How do the same notebook, server and kernel ideas fit into a research computing system?

### Trace a research workflow through Jupyter

Use a prepared [Argonne Leadership Computing Facility (ALCF)](https://www.alcf.anl.gov/) workflow to inspect where the notebook interface, kernel, data and computation live. Trace how the deployment connects a researcher to computing resources, and compare those boundaries with the laptop and classroom examples. We will verify the actual deployment before presenting its configuration, rather than assuming all HPC systems assemble Jupyter in the same way.

Map the relevant Jupyter components and integration points to their source repositories and documentation. Take a workflow requirement or observed limitation and investigate how it becomes an issue, test or reviewed change. Research supplies concrete requirements for the software, not just a reason to show a large computer.

A domain tool from [GeoJupyter](https://geojupyter.org/) is a possible further example: inspect how a research community builds on Jupyter, what it adds, and where that work is developed and reviewed.
