# Inventory selection and source access

The frozen population is the twelve rows of `inventory.json`. This is a purposive
history of dedicated AI integrations and enabling components, not a census or an
adoption ranking. It joins the seven Week 3 component-map subjects (Jupyter AI,
JupyterLite AI, JSD, Server MCP, ACP client, persona manager and frontend commands)
to five contrasting cases: Jupytee, the Jflam browser extension, GPT JupyterLab,
JupyterLab Codeium and Datalayer's MCP server. These capture early chat/magics,
completion, personal experiments, company integration and alternative tool access.

Inclusion requires a public implementation, explicit Jupyter-targeted project
declaration or design proposal, plus an inspectable relationship to notebook use,
chat, completion or AI tool actions. JSD is included as deliberate enabling
infrastructure; its first implementation is not claimed to be its first AI use.
Generic model libraries, protocols and agents are contextual nodes, not counted
as separate Jupyter integrations. Renames and forks within the selected case's
lineage are not automatically additional cases.

Kite's JupyterLab extension is a **prehistory check**, outside the twelve-row
population: its retained README documents machine-learning completion before the
2022 chat examples. Its imported commit history includes unrelated LSP work, so
neither the oldest commit nor its 2020 repository creation is called an AI launch.
JupyterLab's January 2023 GAI proposal also explicitly names Kite and Tabnine as
earlier completion integrations. No claim of the first-ever AI-in-Jupyter tool
is made.

Notebook Intelligence, Pretzel, Chapyter, JELAI, vendor browser extensions,
CodeWhisperer and other packages surfaced in discovery but are outside this
bounded inventory. Their exclusion is not evidence of lesser importance or
different origins. In particular, this inventory cannot estimate the share of
the whole ecosystem originating outside the existing community. The current
Jupyter AI split has additional packages; its announced ten-package list is
retained as a milestone, not silently added to the denominator.

Search results supplied candidates, not historical evidence. Exact spelling is
retained above, including the unsuccessful `jupyterlab-gpt` lead. A guessed
`pbugnion/jupyterlab-gpt` URL returned 404 and is not an inventory entry. The old
Notebook Intelligence namespace redirected and was not used as provenance proof.
No user account or nonpublic organization membership was examined for origins.

Every successful retained GitHub request has its exact URL/parameters and UTC
collection timestamp in the public `source-index.json` (full third-party text snapshots are retained only in the private working archive). Search witness pages deliberately
retain only three results: they are positive/limited-negative checks, not complete
PR collections. Their `total_count` is not a reported activity metric. The
eight-second collection delay used in Week 2 was not part of this collection.
